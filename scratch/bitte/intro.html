What the number means

Transfer entropy measures how much of the uncertainty about a target's next value is removed by the source's recent past, after the target's own recent past has already been accounted for. That last clause is what separates it from correlation. A neuron whose firing is highly predictable from its own history scores zero, however regular it looks; only genuinely new information counts.

For binary data the whole calculation collapses to counting. With one step of target history and one of source history there are exactly eight possible joint states of (y′, y, x) — the target's next bit, its current bit, and the source's bit. Count how often each of the eight occurs and the entropy follows. Everything below is about counting those eight numbers quickly.

From fluorescence to one bit per frame

A calcium indicator does not report spikes. It integrates them and then decays slowly, so the raw trace is a smeared, drifting version of the spike train; thresholding it directly mostly detects was recently active. Taking the first difference along time removes the decay and the drift and leaves a sharp positive transient at each spike. That is what gets thresholded.

The threshold is set per neuron from the median and the median absolute deviation of that neuron's own derivative, so a noisier trace does not simply look busier. Only the rising edge of each run above threshold is kept, so one transient yields one event rather than three adjacent ones. Across all 100 cells this gives 178,139 events, a density of 0.99% — about 0.50 Hz at the 50 Hz frame rate.

Three planes, read from two strings

Here is the step that makes the rest cheap. Pack each spike train one bit per frame, 64 frames to a 64-bit word. The three quantities the estimator needs are then not three arrays but three reads of the same packed memory at different bit offsets: the target's next bit, the target's current bit, and the source's bit. A plane is a shift, not a copy.

The rows below are real: word 1,964 of this recording, frames 125,696 to 125,759, roughly 42 minutes in. Note what the hex says — the future plane is the history plane shifted by exactly one bit.

One AND, one popcount, 64 frames at a time

To count how often a particular joint state occurs, take the plane for each of the three bits, complement the ones that should be zero, AND them together, and count the set bits. A single 64-bit AND tests 64 frames at once, and the population count is one instruction. The complement is free in the same sense: mask & ~P equals mask ^ (mask & P), so once the one-branch is computed the zero-branch costs an XOR and no counting at all, since its total is the parent's total minus the one-branch's.

Eight numbers, then one

Run that over the whole recording and the pair is summarised by eight counts. These are the real ones for cell 45 driving cell 77:

The signal lives in one of them. When cell 45 fires and cell 77 was not already firing, cell 77 fires on the next frame 177 times out of 1,537, which is 11.5%. Without knowing anything about cell 45, cell 77's chance of firing on any given frame is 1.00%. An eleven-fold change in the odds, carried by 177 events out of 179,496 frames.

Transfer entropy weighs that against how often it happens, which is why a large odds ratio on rare events yields a small number of bits:

TE = Σ p(y′, y, x) · log2 [ p(y′ | y, x) ÷ p(y′ | y) ]  =  0.00231 bits per frame

Two of the eight bins are empty, and that is the detector's doing rather than the biology's. Keeping only rising edges means a frame marked as an event cannot be followed by another event, so y′=1 with y=1 never occurs. Those states contribute nothing in either direction, so the estimate rests entirely on the frames where the target was quiet.

Short, medium and long delay

The source bit does not have to come from the immediately preceding frame. The lag is an argument, and since a plane is just a read offset, changing it costs nothing: the same packed data is read a few bits further along. Three delays were run over all 9,900 pairs.

That collapse is the expected shape. At 20 ms per frame a monosynaptic delay is far shorter than one frame, so a real influence appears at the first lag and nowhere else. A lag profile that peaked at 100 or 500 ms would more likely indicate slow shared drive than a connection.

What it cost

The run covered 39,800 ordered pairs, because the 100 rotation surrogates are estimated alongside the 100 real signals in the same pass.

What these numbers do not say

  • 8,076 pairs out of 9,900 clear the null at one frame. That is not 8,076 synapses. This kind of culture fires in network-wide bursts — 0.3% of frames here contain ten or more simultaneous events, and one contains 98 — and a burst makes every pair mutually predictive whether or not they are wired together. Pairwise transfer entropy cannot separate a direct connection from shared drive. The standard remedy is to condition on frames of low population activity, which the bit representation makes cheap: it is one more mask ANDed into the root.
  • The strongest edges are not simply the busiest cells. Worth checking, and it does not hold: cell 45, the most frequent source among the top edges, ranks 83rd of 100 by firing rate. Per-neuron thresholding leaves all 100 rates between 0.88% and 1.08%, so rate cannot drive the asymmetry.
  • 0.0023 bits is small in absolute terms even at 35× the null ceiling. It is an average over every frame, and the informative frames are the 1% where something happened.
  • One step of history each. Both the target and the source get a single frame of past. Longer histories are supported, and the method stays faster than a scalar count up to about seven total history bits, past which the state space outgrows the 64-fold packing and a plain per-sample histogram wins.
